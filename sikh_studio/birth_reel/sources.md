# Written sources and confidence tags

1. Max Arthur Macauliffe, *The Sikh Religion*, Volume I (1909), Life of Guru Nanak, Chapter I, pp. 1-2, https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Sikh_Religion,_its_gurus,_sacred_writings_and_authors_Vol_1.djvu/97 .
   - 1469, Talwandi, father Kalu (village accountant), mother Tripta, **birth traditions** involving Daulatan and Hardial.
   - The later narrative includes chill weather but this is not a meteorological record; NO precise conditions will be claimed.

2. *Janamsakhi Tradition: An Analytical Study*, Sikh Missionary Society, https://www.sikhmissionarysociety.org/sms/smspublications/JanamsakhiTraditionAnAnalyticalStudy.pdf .
   - Compares variant Janamsakhi accounts, includes Vaisakh Sudi 3, explains conflicting traditions; source-critical framing.

3. Khushwant Singh, *A History of the Sikhs*, Volume I (1963), https://discoversikhism.com/pdf/a_history_of_the_sikhs-volume1.pdf .
   - Historical context and agreement on 1469 and parents. Scholarly interpretation, not an eyewitness birth document.

Evidence classes:
- Historical: Year 1469; rural Punjab village Talwandi; named parents; Kalu as accountant; Nanak becomes first Sikh Guru.
- Later tradition: Daulatan as midwife; Hardial astrologer; any baby's unusual voice or future predictions. The reel does **not** present these as proven events.
- Reconstruction only: Architecture, lighting, garments, props, gestures, animal and street movements. Materials and shapes are approximations rather than museum-researched exact reproductions.
